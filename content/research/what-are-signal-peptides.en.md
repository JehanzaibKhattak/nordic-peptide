Peptides are short chains of amino acids. In skincare, a handful of them are used because they are small enough to be formulated into stable serums and creams and have a reasonable body of cosmetic research behind them.

## The families you'll actually see

**Signal peptides** such as palmitoyl tripeptide-1 and palmitoyl tetrapeptide-7 (sold together as Matrixyl 3000) are used in products aimed at the look of firmness and fine lines.

**Neuro-modulating peptides** like acetyl hexapeptide-8 (Argireline) show up in products targeting the appearance of expression lines.

**Carrier peptides** — copper tripeptide-1 (GHK-Cu) is the well-known one — are used in soothing, skin-conditioning formulas.

## What to look for on a label

- The peptide named in full on the INCI list, not hidden behind a "complex".
- A stated concentration. Many studies use 2–10%; below that the amount may be cosmetic in the other sense.
- A pH in the 5–6.5 range for most peptides, which is also where skin is comfortable.

## Realistic expectations

Peptide skincare is about appearance and skin feel: smoother texture, better hydration, a fresher look. It is not a medical treatment and shouldn't be marketed as one.
